Use the attached master CV as the sole source of candidate facts. The supplied verified job description, company research and this formatting specification guide relevance and presentation. Examples below illustrate style only: never copy their names, dates, grades, locations, skills, memberships, numbers or achievements unless the master CV independently supports them. Preserve the master's real job titles. Do not claim an 80–100% match when the evidence does not support it.

CV Audit and Tailoring Process


Act as an experienced UK recruiter with 20 years of experience and versed in the art of writing CVs and the psychology of advertising. Our goal is to tailor my CV and achieve an extremely high match (80 to 100%) one-page CV following these steps:

1. Count the competency words or phrases in the JD and perform this math on them. Find the average and attach a percentage to it (That is how we test match). eg., “If a competency word or phrase appears more than once, it could hold more weight/ percentage than something that appears once”. Unless that word appears in the experience required, duties or what we are looking for section — then if it appears once, it is still very important.” Rank the words and store all in a Temporary word or phrase bank. (More weighted words can appear in the CV once or twice during the tailoring process)

2. Review the job description and find matching words or phrases(whether competency or not) in my master resume. Temporarily hold those words or phrases in the word bank. Before deleting or rewriting any master CV content, check whether that change removes an existing JD keyword match. If it does, preserve that word elsewhere when it is accurate and relevant. No existing relevant JD keyword should disappear from the tailored CV unless the associated evidence is genuinely unsuitable or there is a justified space trade-off.

3. Analyse my master resume for other skills or phrases present in the JD that have been worded differently. Rewrite them into a phrase or word so they match the JD, and hold them in the temporary word/phrase bank.

4. For speculative applications, follow the speculative branch below instead of JD extraction and scoring. Use supplied company research to identify relevance.

Steps expanded
Step 1

Step 1 — Competency Frequency and Percentage Weighting
Extract every competency word or phrase from the JD, including relevant technical skills, responsibilities, commercial capabilities and behavioural requirements.
Count how many times each competency appears in the JD, including repetitions.
Calculate the total number of competency occurrences across the JD, including repetitions.
Calculate each competency's percentage weight:
Competency Weight (%) = (Competency Frequency ÷ Total Competency Occurrences) × 100
The sum of all competency weights must equal 100%.
If a competency appears at least once in the CV, award its entire percentage weight. Additional mentions do not increase the mathematical score.
A competency may appear more than once where it naturally strengthens the CV and makes the capability easier for recruiters to identify.
Do not force repetition or sacrifice writing quality to increase keyword density.
Distinguish demonstrated competencies from unsupported requirements and irrelevant employment or benefits terminology.
Calculate total weighted CV coverage by adding the weights of all competencies evidenced in the CV.
Apply exactly the same word bank and weighting methodology when scoring the master CV and tailored CV.



Step 2
Competency Matching Rules
Classify each JD competency against the master CV as:
Exact Match: The same competency word or phrase is explicitly present.
Equivalent Match: The competency is demonstrably present but expressed using different terminology.
Missing: The competency is neither explicitly present nor sufficiently evidenced.
For equivalent matches, identify the supporting evidence in the master CV and rewrite the relevant statement using the JD's terminology without exaggerating the original meaning.
Calculate two coverage scores:
Exact Keyword Coverage: Sum the percentage weights of competencies appearing explicitly in the CV.
Evidence-Based Competency Coverage: Sum the percentage weights of exact matches and evidenced equivalent matches.
Count each competency only once, regardless of how many times it appears in the CV.
Never treat an unsupported competency as a match.

Step 3

Keyword Coverage Preservation — Mandatory Safeguard
The master CV establishes the minimum evidence-based competency coverage baseline.
After tailoring, calculate the weighted coverage of both documents using the same competency bank.
The tailored CV should achieve equal or higher weighted exact-keyword coverage than the master CV wherever feasible, while remaining accurate, naturally written and within one page.
If the tailored CV scores lower:
Identify every lost competency and its percentage weight.
Determine whether it can be restored through JD-aligned wording in Work Experience, Projects or Skills.
Prioritise restoring competencies with the highest weighting.
Recalculate the coverage after each revision.
If full restoration is impossible without damaging readability or accuracy, explain the necessary trade-offs.
Do not consider the document fully validated until all avoidable losses have been addressed.
Never add unsupported skills or irrelevant keywords merely to achieve a numerical target.

Extra
Competency Extraction and Counting Rules
Extract meaningful competency words and established skill phrases from the JD.
Prefer complete competency phrases where individual words would lose their meaning.
Avoid counting a word separately when it is already part of the same extracted competency phrase, unless the word independently represents another requirement.
Treat straightforward grammatical variations as the same competency, such as "analyse", "analysing" and "analysis", where they refer to the same skill.
Preserve genuinely distinct requirements, such as "market fundamentals", "forecasting" and "risk analysis".
Count each occurrence consistently across the JD.
Exclude salary, benefits, employment conditions and administrative wording unless directly relevant to the candidate's qualifications.
Record the extracted terms, frequencies and percentage weights before tailoring begins.
The same extraction and matching rules must be applied to every CV being compared.





Now that we have identified all the words and phrases required, we actually move to tailoring the CV.

Professional Summary:
This must not exceed 70 words or three clean sentences. A perfect example will be who I am - what I do or have done - what I am looking for that matches the JD or makes me the relevant candidate. Eg for a role in Finance we can write “Chemical and Petroleum Engineering professional with hands-on experience delivering Ghana’s first $350M LNG terminal through commercial analysis, quantitative modelling and project controls. Builds financial and technical models in Excel, Python and MATLAB, with award-winning design and business planning work. Now building towards roles analysing or trading energy and commodity markets”

A placeholder version will look like: [xyz] professional with hands-on experience [something I have done that the recruiter may be looking for (e.g., project, job, course, development, etc.)] through [x, y, and z skills that appeared the most or most relevant that I have]. Builds [stuff that I build and do that relate to the job description, use numbers like a minimalist but confident if required] in [where I build them, tools, competencies, etc.] with award-winning design [Achievements that set me apart and showcase competency or more(making me memorable)]. Now building towards roles [maybe the industry, field, or projects the company works on without explicitly mentioning company name]

Use the word bank to guide relevance, not dictate wording. Select only the two or three most defining competencies that naturally support the candidate's professional story. The summary is not responsible for achieving keyword coverage. Distribute the remaining JD competencies across Work Experience, Projects and Skills, where they can be demonstrated with evidence.

Professional Summary must immediately tell the reader, “this is who I am looking for”, assuming they were using the JD as a marking scheme.  Do not fake titles to meet this goal; my last role was in operations analytics, and MSc BSc. I do xyz to tell them I am who they are looking for. etc


Hard rules for professional Summary
Professional Summary — Narrative Quality Control (Mandatory)
The professional summary is an introduction to the candidate, NOT a keyword optimisation section.
Follow this exact narrative structure:
Who I am: My professional or academic identity, supported by my most relevant experience.
What I have done: One or two defining capabilities or achievements that establish credibility for the role.
Where I am going: My career direction and how it naturally connects to the position.
Strict rules:
Maximum 70 words and three clean sentences.
Prioritise clarity, natural flow and professional identity over keyword coverage.
Include only JD keywords that fit organically into the narrative. Never force a keyword into the summary solely because it has a high weighting.
Do not list competencies, tools or responsibilities merely to increase the match score.
Avoid sentences that read like collections of disconnected skills joined by commas.
Every sentence must communicate a distinct idea and advance the narrative.
Use the rest of the CV, particularly Projects, Work Experience and Skills, to achieve keyword coverage.
Do not repeat a competency in the summary simply because it appears frequently in the JD.
Mandatory quality test:
After drafting the summary, temporarily ignore the JD and keyword bank. Read the summary as an independent professional introduction.
Ask:
Does it sound like a real professional describing their background and career direction?
Does each sentence have a clear purpose?
Would it still read naturally if the JD did not exist?
Are any words included primarily to satisfy an ATS rather than communicate meaningful information?
If any sentence fails these tests, rewrite it before proceeding.
Priority rule: Narrative coherence and credibility take precedence over keyword density in the professional summary. Recover any omitted relevant keywords through evidenced Work Experience, Projects or Skills instead.
DO not use “combining”


Execute a professional Summary.


Experience and Projects Floating Goal
Here, analyse all my work experience and projects from my master CV and select projects directly relevant to the role, the company, and their projects or cause/values.
Analysing the company may require research into the company, what they do, their business model, how they make money, what the role may involve, the day-to-day, and what is required of a candidate to thrive in such a position. We use this to select the important experience and projects to prioritise. Then we rewrite them using the proposed structure in each section whilst incorporating the words from the temporary word bank (A word can appear once or twice in the CV; it doesn’t matter how many times it appears in the CV as long as it is covered and also evidenced)


Work Experience:

Use this similar structure — varying them, of course
Action verb + technical activity + scope/context + business benefit
Action verb + responsibility + methodology + quantified outcome
Action verb + responsibility + collaboration/method + quantified outcome
Action verb + stakeholder/team + analytical method + commercial outcome

An example of each will be:
“·       Applied project control principles for schedule, cost, and risk tracking across work packages, giving stakeholders real-time visibility into delivery progress
·       Coordinated multidisciplinary work packages using agile methodologies to meet key performance indicators (KPIs) of 80% on-time delivery
·       Managed safety tracker actions, collaborating with cross-functional teams to close out 80% of outstanding Hazard actions
·       Liaised with the procurement team through cost analysis and data-driven support for vendor negotiations to reduce maintenance costs of engineering equipment by 3–5%.

The style has five defining characteristics:
Strong opening verbs: Applied, Coordinated, Managed, Liaised.
Specific responsibilities: Names the actual work performed rather than generic competencies.
Methods embedded naturally: Uses phrases such as using, through, collaborating with and to.
Results at the end: Quantified achievements or clear business benefits conclude the statements.
Concise professional English: One sentence per bullet, no personal pronouns, minimal adjectives and no unnecessary explanation.

Now that we have the structure sorted, let's talk about tailoring.

When tailoring, first identify how many bullets I currently have.
Review all bullets and rank them by alignment to the job description. Check if they can be rephrased to meet the temporary word or phrase bank and evidence the skills. If a bullet cannot meet it explicitly, eliminate it. We do not mind an experience having one or two bullets if the rest do not match and cannot work with replacing the word bank. (Space is crucial to us).
Once this is done, arrange remaining bullets in order of strong match (strong match can be classed by having words from the temporary word bank with a high percentage or competency in recommended, essential or what we are looking for sections)

Projects:
Now projects are quite important because that is where we showcase most skills. We currently have limited work experience. Following the floating goal, pick relevant projects. We can choose a number of projects based on space (1-page requirement).

structure could be “Past-tense action verb + specific technical deliverable + quantified scale/context (where relevant) + methodology, tools or analytical techniques + measurable outcome or business purpose.”
An example will be “Techno-Economic Modelling (Excel & Python) | UON | 2024
·       Built a 5-scenario capex and opex financial model (£16.8M–£32.2M investment range) through equipment costing, capital cost breakdown, and a 20-year cash flow projection to deliver 255% ROI and 6-year payback sensitivity analysis”

One comprehensive bullet per project that meets the floating experience goal.

Skills:
Skills section follows a different writing structure from Work Experience and Projects. It is category-led, compact and application-focused, showing both what you know and what you can do with it. It however should still respect the floating company analysis goal and incorporate the temporary word bank phrases and words. By the end of CV we aim to naturally include and evidence word bank to achieve that 80 to 100% tailoring efficiency.

Skills Writing Style/ Structure
Write each skills bullet as one compact, category-led statement following:
Bold competency category + colon + specific tools, techniques or demonstrated activities + practical application or business purpose (where relevant).
Preserve the original CV's three natural writing variations:
Tool-led: Category: tools and technologies + "for" + analytical or technical application.
Technique-led: Category: concise list of relevant methods and competencies.
Application-led: Category: active verb + specific activity + "to support", "to evaluate" or "to forecast" + practical purpose.
Competency-led: Active verb, specific domain, to achieve what? eg. Experienced in designing oil and gas plants from field xyz (competency-based skills are based on the company and work experience, floating goal)
Use short, technically specific phrases rather than full achievement statements. Prioritise JD-relevant competencies supported by the Master CV.
Maintain approximately four category-led bullets, the original writing density, bold labels and professional UK English.
Avoid keyword stuffing, generic proficiency claims ("excellent", "highly skilled", "proficient in"), personal pronouns, invented capabilities and unnecessary explanations.

Egs.: ·       Computing: Advanced Excel, Python (Pandas & NumPy), and MATLAB for quantitative analysis and data modelling

FINAL VALIDATION — MANDATORY QUALITY GATE
Do not generate the final CV until all checks below have been completed.
1. Competency coverage
Compare the completed CV against the weighted JD competency bank.
Verify that existing relevant JD keywords from the master CV have been preserved wherever possible.
Confirm that differently worded but equivalent competencies have been converted into JD-aligned terminology without changing their meaning.
Calculate weighted competency coverage for the master CV and tailored CV using the same methodology.
Investigate every relevant competency lost during tailoring.
Do not add unsupported skills or irrelevant employment-benefit keywords to increase the score.
2. Writing quality
Confirm that the professional summary follows Who I Am → What I Have Done → Where I Am Going.
Ensure the summary reads naturally without reference to the JD.
Check that work experience bullets follow the specified action–method–outcome structures.
Ensure each project has one comprehensive, relevant bullet.
Confirm that skills are concise, category-led and application-focused.
Remove keyword stuffing, unnecessary repetition and generic claims.
3. Evidence and accuracy
Every technical skill, project claim, responsibility and quantified outcome must be supported by the master CV or supplied evidence.
Never invent experience, measurable results or technical expertise.
Distinguish actual achievements from estimates, simulations and planned functionality.
4. Formatting
Verify the Word document follows the exact page setup, typography, margins, spacing and section structure specified in this prompt.
Confirm that the CV fits on one page without reducing readability.
Preserve working hyperlinks.
5. Final approval
Only generate the completed CV once all checks pass.
If a requirement cannot be satisfied, explain the trade-off rather than silently omitting it.
Report the calculated competency coverage separately from any external ATS score.
Never claim an externally verified ATS match percentage without evidence.
